When it cannot wait

When a damp spot starts spreading

A drip into a bucket during a dry week is a nuisance. Water that returns to the same place with every storm, a stain that has crossed its pencil line twice, or insulation that feels heavy and cold is another matter, because wet wood and wet insulation stay wet long after the sky clears.

The EPA's mold guide opens with the same advice: water problems need attention as soon as they turn up, and wet material needs drying all the way through. Any one of those three signs is enough to dial (551) 210-7708 and ask when a roofer can look; jot down the date of each stain first.

What the work involves

How one shingle comes out and another goes in

The row above is lifted just far enough to reach the nails holding the damaged shingle. Those nails are pulled, the old shingle slides out, and the new one slips in with its nails set in the nailing strip, where the next row will cover the heads. The upper tabs press back down, and the neighbors get a look, since a cracked shingle often has a cracked neighbor.

FEMA's technical fact sheet on asphalt shingles calls for roofing nails rather than staples, underlayment beneath, and a check that the tabs have sealed once the sun has warmed them.

Stop and get help first if

  • Water is running from a ceiling fixture or a wall plug: step out of the room, leave the switch alone, and call 911 from a dry spot.
  • Part of a ceiling has bellied downward and looks heavy: clear the room beneath it, and call 911 if the plaster or board begins to drop.
  • A wire lies across the roof, the yard or a tree branch: assume it is energized, keep about two pole spans (30 feet) between you and it, and dial 1-800-436-7734 for PSE&G, or 911 if anyone is close to it.
  • A relative or neighbor offers to climb up and look while the roof is wet, frosty or steep: talk them out of it, and call 911 only if someone has already fallen.

Close to home

The Census Bureau's 2020-2024 survey puts the middle North Arlington house at 1954, give or take 2 years, and roughly 69 percent of the borough's 6,900 or so homes went up in 1959 or before. Houses from that stretch may be wearing several layers of shingles, so ask how many are up there before anyone quotes a patch.

Teterboro Airport, the NOAA station about 6 miles away in the 1991-2020 normals, records July as its wettest month at 4.45 inches, with June and August each at 4.25. On average 1.5 July days deliver at least an inch of rain, the sort of downpour that finds a gap in a shingle course.

More than half of the occupied homes here are rented: 3,699 of 6,649, or 55.6 percent, with a margin of about 441 on the renter count, in the same Census estimate. Anyone renting who spots a stain contacts the landlord or property manager first.

What moves the price

What changes the number on a patch quote

How many shingles are damaged counts for less than how far the water got. A wet shingle is a short job; a soaked deck means boards cut out and replaced. Height and pitch change the setup, and so do the pieces nearby, such as a chimney, a vent pipe or a valley, each of which brings flashing to check.

A house already carrying two or three layers of old shingle gives the roofer more to work through. The Department of Energy's repair brief passes along a guide's line for the bigger question: past 10 percent of the old roof needing repair, price a new one beside the patch.

Paperwork pointer

Paperwork for a repair

A detached home with one or two families sits under a state list of ordinary maintenance, and patching the roof covering is on it, so no permit application is needed. Attached homes, larger buildings and each town's office are covered on the local rules page.
