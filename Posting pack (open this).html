Services

Roof repair — Following a ceiling stain, a lifted shingle or a gap in the courses back to its source, then laying out what a repair involves. Roof leak investigation — Working out where water enters and how it travels inside: shingles, a vent boot, a valley, a chimney joint or a flat-roof seam. Roof replacement — Tear-off and a new covering, with underlayment, flashing and edge details described in writing so quotes can be compared. Storm damage repair — Wind, limb and water damage to shingles, flashing and gutters, with notes and photos that help with an insurance claim. Roof inspection — Ridge, edges, flashing and valleys checked from the roof, and the underside of the boards checked from the attic. Flat roof repair — Seams, drains, edges and ponding on low-slope roofs over additions, porches and small multi-unit buildings. Flat roof re-covering — Replacing a worn membrane with single-ply, built-up or modified bitumen, depending on the building. Gutter repair and cleaning — Clearing, re-hanging and re-pitching gutters, and reconnecting downspouts so water ends away from the wall. Gutter and downspout replacement — New runs, corners and downspouts sized to the roof area, with the fascia behind them checked first. Ice dam assessment — Looking at the eaves, attic floor and ventilation to see why ice forms, and which steps address the cause. Chimney flashing repair — Base flashing, counterflashing and mortar joints where a chimney or a wall meets the roof.

Questions and answers

Q: How do I look up a roofer's New Jersey registration? A: The Division of Consumer Affairs offers an online verification site, and its Business Search includes home improvement contractors. The number is meant to be shown on ads, vehicles and business paperwork, so ask the roofer for it. Q: Is a permit application required to reroof a New Jersey house? A: On a detached house with one or two families, the state's maintenance alert lists roof covering repair and full replacement as ordinary maintenance, so no permit application is needed. The alert adds that the label does not excuse a job from the building code. Attached houses go to the town office. Q: What does a written home improvement contract list? A: Above $500, the contract needs signatures from everyone involved and has to list the seller and buyer, what the job is and the main materials, the full price, when the work starts and ends, and any promise about products or labor. Later changes go in writing too. Q: What are the first steps after storm damage to a roof? A: The state insurance department asks homeowners to report the loss promptly, make temporary repairs, keep receipts and photograph damage before disposing of it. The choice of contractor stays with the homeowner, whatever the insurer recommends. Q: Is there a three-day window to cancel a home sales contract? A: Under the FTC's Cooling-Off Rule a buyer can cancel certain at-home sales within three business days. Emergencies and visits the buyer asked for in order to get repairs are among the exceptions, so the written contract is the document to read. Q: Who handles building paperwork in Kearny? A: Kearny's Construction Code Enforcement front office is at (201) 955-7880, Monday to Friday 8:00 to 4:00, with an online permit portal. The page does not mention roofing, so ask the office about a specific job.
